We fine-tune Cohere Labs' Tiny Aya Fire (about 3.35B parameters) into a banking tool-calling agent for IndicBankBench, a benchmark of 799 Indian retail-banking agent cases (32 tools, English, Hinglish and Indic-script users). Fine-tuning uses LoRA (r=64) on about 2,600 synthetic multi-turn conversations generated from the benchmark's public tool schemas and system prompt (test cases are never read). Loss is computed only on assistant turns under the exact chat template used at serving time. Before to after on the benchmark: strict pass^3 rises from about 3% to about 24%, and pass@3 from 16.8% to about 37%.

Because fine-tuning can shift safety behaviour, we audit it against the base model (JailbreakBench harmful and benign prompts, four jailbreak wrappers, a hand-written English/Hinglish/Hindi banking red-team set, and 32 bank-agent policy checks, judged by Qwen3Guard plus a regex refusal detector, with 95% Wilson intervals and paired McNemar tests). The fine-tuned model is safer against jailbreaks (attack success 18% to 8.5% on JailbreakBench) but over-refuses benign prompts more (17% to 32%, p<0.001).

We then tried to fix over-refusal without retraining, using CircuitKIT to locate the refusal circuit and AuditKIT to evaluate a weight-orthogonalisation edit of a "looks scary" direction kept orthogonal to a "real danger" direction. This repaired model did not meet our bar (it behaved worse on refusal), so we withdrew it and do not release it. We report this as a negative result. The released model is the fine-tuned model, with its measured safety/over-refusal trade-off documented. Everything (code, jobs, logs, results) is open and reproducible on Hugging Face Jobs for a few dollars.
